Ask a room full of professionals whether they gossip at work, and most will look at their
shoes. 🙈 Gossip has a reputation problem - and it is not entirely undeserved.

But step back, and the definition is neutral. Gossip is simply talk about people. The question
is never whether we do it - we do, constantly. The question is what it is for. 🤔

**Positive Gossiping** is a way to point that instinct somewhere useful. 🙌

## 🤔 What it actually is

Positive Gossiping is a structured, timed activity for three people. One tells a story about a
person who mattered to them. One asks the questions. One watches, takes notes and protects the
tone of the room. 🎭

Ten minutes per round, and then everyone moves. 🔄

It sounds almost too simple to be worth scheduling. That is part of why it works.

## 👥 Three roles, one round

🕵️ **The gossiper** speaks. Not about a rival or an irritation, but about someone who left a
positive mark - a person who taught them something new, opened a door, or steadied them
through a rough patch. 💛

🎤 **The interviewer** asks. The questions are not idle. They probe how that person's guidance
changed something, and what the gossiper actually carried away from it. The point is to turn a
warm memory into a transferable lesson.

🛡️ **The observer and prompter** watches and keeps the room honest. They take notes, watch the
clock, and hold one line firmly: no fault-finding, no passive aggression, no blame or insult
aimed at any person, caste, creed, religion, colour, community, organisation or institution.

## ⏱️ How a ten-minute round runs

1. **Set the clock.** Ten minutes.
2. **The gossiper tells** a positive story about someone who mattered.
3. **The interviewer asks** questions that surface the learning.
4. **The observer notes** the key points and protects the tone.
5. **At the buzzer,** the observer summarises what the round produced. 🔔

Then the seats rotate. 🔄 The observer becomes the gossiper, the gossiper becomes the
interviewer, and the interviewer becomes the observer. After three rounds - roughly half an
hour - every participant has played every role. 🎯

The rotation is the design, not a detail. Telling, questioning and watching are three
different skills, and most people are quietly much better at one than at the others.

## ⚠️ The one rule that makes it work

Keep it away from your current workplace. 🚫🏢

Do not run this about the colleagues sitting next to you. Reach instead for school or college,
for a teacher, trainer or coach, or for an event or gathering that had nothing to do with your
present employer. 🎓

This is not caution for its own sake. The moment the material is live and current, the exercise
stops being about learning and starts being about office politics. Distance is what makes it
safe - and safety is what makes people honest.

## 🌟 Why it works

- 🎧 **It builds a listening culture.** You learn to hear, rather than wait your turn to speak.
- 🧠 **It turns reflection into habit.** Every round produces a real lesson, stated out loud.
- 🤝 **It strengthens trust.** Positive stories make a team feel human to itself.
- ⚡ **It costs nothing.** Three people, half an hour, no tools required.
- 🌍 **It is inclusive by design.** The guardrails keep it respectful for everyone in the room.

## 🚀 Try it with your team

All you need is three people, a timer and a willingness to listen. Run it in a huddle, in a
training session, or in a Friday team meeting. ☕

You may be surprised how much you learn about the people you work with - and how much they
learn about themselves.
