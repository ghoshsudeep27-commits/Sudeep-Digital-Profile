India is no longer just an AI market. It is becoming the AI engine room. ⚡

Global AI companies are now renting capacity in Indian data centres to serve customers far beyond
India - because power and capacity delays elsewhere are forcing them to spread their compute across
countries. That is the headline from a Times of India report this week, and it matters far more for
your team than it first appears.

## Two numbers tell the story

- **Yotta.** Sunil Gupta, co-founder and CEO, says they are already hosting international AI
  customers, with large capacities contracted - and those workloads serve the world, not just local
  users.
- **CtrlS.** A conversation that starts at 20MW of capacity can quickly turn into 100MW or more.
  As founder and CEO Sridhar Pinnapureddy puts it: *"AI is compressing the time available to respond
  while increasing the scale we need to build for."*

Read that again. Compressing the time to respond. Increasing the scale to build.

## This is a skills problem, not just a server problem 🧠

When compute scales five-fold, the humans around it have to scale too - in judgement, in critical
thinking, in the ability to ask an AI tool the right question and then challenge the answer it gives
back.

Higher power densities and advanced cooling can be planned alongside land, transmission and future
expansion. That is an engineering and capital problem, and India is solving it fast.

Judgement cannot be procured. It has to be trained.

> A data centre can be built in months. A team that can think critically alongside AI takes longer -
> and it decides whether the compute actually pays off.

## Where the gap actually sits

In the workshops I run, the pattern is consistent. Teams adopt tools quickly and then stall, because
the constraint was never access. It is knowing when the model is confidently wrong, what a safe
prompt looks like for client data, and how to keep a human decision in the loop where it matters.

That is not a technology project. It is a capability one, and it is solvable.
