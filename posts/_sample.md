Ask a leadership team whether their staff use AI, and you will usually get one of two
answers. Either "we have not rolled anything out yet", or "we use Copilot".

Both answers are usually wrong in the same way.

## The tool nobody approved

In almost every workshop I run, the same thing surfaces within the first hour. Somebody
in the room is using a personal ChatGPT account to draft client emails. Somebody else
is pasting financial data into a free summariser. Nobody approved it, and in most cases
nobody knows about it.

This is **shadow AI**, and it is the single most common finding in an AI-readiness review.

## Why it happens

It is not defiance. It is usually efficiency. A person has a task, the approved tool is
not available or not useful for it, and a consumer tool solves the problem in ninety
seconds. The organisation finds out later, if at all.

> The risk is not that your team is using AI. It is that you do not know where.

## What actually reduces it

- **Give people a legitimate path.** Shadow AI thrives where the approved option is slower than the personal one.
- **Be specific about data.** "Do not paste sensitive data" fails. "Client names, pricing and personal health information never go into a public model" works.
- **Check the score honestly.** Teams consistently over-rate themselves on tools and under-rate themselves on governance.

## The part that is easy to miss

Closing the gap is not a technology project. It is a capability one. People need to know
what a safe prompt looks like, what a plausible-sounding wrong answer looks like, and when
to stop and ask.

That is a training problem, and it is solvable in an afternoon.
